Iran grows almonds in the cooler northwestern provinces — Tabriz, Qazvin, Hamedan — and the Iranian almond (badam) is a distinct product from the California-origin almond that dominates most international retail shelves. Smaller, denser, higher in oil and with a more pronounced almond flavour, the Iranian almond occupies the premium end of the global almond trade, and the "golden" selection — uniformly sized, light-skinned, intact kernels — is the grade that international roasters, confectioners and gift-box assemblers specify. ## Golden Almonds Direct Sourcing Iran: the grade that defines the trade

Almonds are traded in two forms: in-shell (the nut with its shell intact, sold by weight including shell) and kernel (the shelled nut, sold by net kernel weight). The international trade is overwhelmingly a kernel trade — buyers want usable kernel, not shell — and the grades within the kernel trade are defined by size, colour, breakage rate, and defect tolerance. A **Golden Almonds Direct Sourcing Iran** order specifies:

- **Form**: natural kernel (skin on), blanched (skin removed), or sliced / slivered / diced.
- **Size**: counted per ounce (e.g. 18/20, 20/22, 23/25) — the smaller the count, the larger the kernel. Iranian golden almonds typically run 23/25 to 27/30; the largest Iranian kernels reach 18/20.
- **Colour**: light amber and light ("golden" is the trade term for the light-amber selection with uniform colour and no blemish).
- **Breakage tolerance**: typically 2-5% by weight; lower breakage rates command a premium.
- **Defect tolerance**: split, doubled, chipped, and discoloured kernels, usually capped at 3-5% combined.
- **Moisture**: 5-6% maximum for export; above 7% the kernel is at mould risk in transit.

The direct-sourcing case for Iranian almonds mirrors the case for other Iranian food exports: eliminating the trading-house margin between the grower and the buyer. Iranian almonds pass through a grower, a local consolidator, a Tabriz or Tehran wholesale market, and often a destination-market importer before reaching a roaster or gift assembler. A direct sourcing relationship with Palude removes the export-trading-house layer and, for volume buyers, the wholesale-market layer. The replacement function — quality verification — is delivered through on-site kernel selection at the packing facility, optical sorting for defects and discolouration, and a photographic pack approval before the container ships.

## The Iranian almond versus the California almond: a buyer's comparison

International buyers usually compare Iranian almonds against California Nonpareil and California Mission grades. The comparison is real and worth making explicitly:

- **Size**: California Nonpareil produces larger, more uniform kernels than the Iranian almond. A buyer whose application is a uniformly-sized retail pack often prefers California.
- **Flavour and oil**: the Iranian almond is denser, higher in oil, and has a more pronounced almond aroma. A buyer whose application is almond paste, marzipan, almond butter, or a flavour-forward confection usually prefers Iranian.
- **Price**: Iranian almonds trade at a premium to California for equivalent sizes, because Iranian supply is smaller and the grower base is fragmented. The premium is the price of the flavour difference.
- **Supply reliability**: California supply is industrial-scale and year-round. Iranian supply is seasonal (harvest September-October) and carry-stock depletes through the summer.

A buyer's choice is not "Iranian is better" — it is "which application, which flavour profile, which price point." We supply Iranian almonds to buyers who have made that comparison and chosen the Iranian flavour profile for their application.

## Packaging for almond and gift-box export

Our standard export packaging:

- **Natural kernels**: 12.5 kg or 25 kg vacuum-sealed foil pouches in cartons; or 25 kg nitrogen-flushed food-grade bags. Palletised for full-container orders.
- **Blanched / sliced / slivered**: 12.5 kg vacuum cartons (the higher-value processed formats are vacuum-packed to protect the cut surfaces from oxidation).
- **Gift boxes**: retailer-specified carton counts (typically 6, 12, or 24 boxes per shipping carton); palletised for full-container orders.
- **Almond paste / butter**: 20 kg food-grade pails or 200 kg drums for industrial confectionery and marzipan manufacturers.

Almonds are a dry cargo and ship in dry (non-reefer) containers, but they are sensitive to moisture and to strong odours — they must not share a container with a cargo that off-gasses (bitumen drums, for example). For buyers consolidating almonds with other Iranian food exports, almonds and pistachios share a dry-container set-point comfortably; almonds and dates do not, because the dates' refrigeration requirement would over-chill the almonds. ## Lead time, MOQ and the harvest cycle

The Iranian almond harvest runs from late August through October, with the Tabriz and Qazvin harvests peaking in September-October. New-crop kernels enter the wholesale market in October-November. Carry-stock is available year-round but depletes and reprices upward from June onwards.

- **Minimum order**: 1 metric ton for natural kernel export; 500 kg for blanched / sliced / slivered; 1,000 gift boxes for the gift-box programme.
- **Lead time**: 14-21 business days from proforma acceptance for stock kernels; 25-35 days for processed formats and gift-box assembly.
- **Incoterms**: FOB Bandar Abbas, CFR/CIF destination port, DAP where the destination permits.
