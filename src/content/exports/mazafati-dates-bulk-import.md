Mazafati is the Iranian date variety that built the country's fresh-date export trade. Soft, dark, moist and almost caramel in flavour, the Mazafati date (called Mozafati in some transliterations) is the date that Middle Eastern, South Asian and increasingly European retail customers reach for at Ramadan and year-round. For a B2B buyer, the Mazafati category is defined by two growing regions — Bam and Jiroft — and by a packaging discipline called selective layering that separates export-grade dates from domestic-grade fruit. This page is the sourcing framework Palude applies to **Mazafati Dates Bam vs Jiroft Wholesale** orders, to **Selective Layering Mazafati Dates Bulk Import** specifications, and to the broader Iranian dried-fruit and stuffed-date programmes international buyers run alongside their date volumes.

## Mazafati Dates Bam vs Jiroft Wholesale: the origin decision

Mazafati dates are grown in a narrow geography: the Bam and Jiroft oases in Kerman province, southern Iran. The two origins produce visually similar fruit but with consistent, measurable differences that matter to a bulk buyer.

**Bam-origin Mazafati** is the premium origin. The Bam oasis, at roughly 1,000 metres elevation with hot days and cool nights, produces a date with higher moisture (22-26%), a darker skin, a softer chew, and the caramel-raisin flavour profile that the Mazafati reputation is built on. Bam dates command a 10-20% premium over Jiroft in the wholesale trade and are the origin specified by Middle Eastern distributors selling into Ramadan retail.

**Jiroft-origin Mazafati** is grown at lower elevation, ripens slightly earlier, and produces a date with marginally lower moisture (20-23%), a slightly lighter skin, and a firmer bite. Jiroft is not a lesser date — it is a different date. For a buyer supplying a price-sensitive South Asian market, an industrial date-paste user, or a food service distributor, Jiroft is often the correct choice because the price-per-ton is lower and the firmer texture survives rougher handling.

When we quote **Mazafati Dates Bam vs Jiroft Wholesale**, we quote both origins with the moisture range and the price delta stated, and we let the buyer's application decide. The single most common mistake new buyers make is specifying "Mazafati" without naming the origin — and receiving whichever origin the supplier has most of that week.

## Selective Layering Mazafati Dates Bulk Import: the packaging discipline

**Selective Layering Mazafati Dates Bulk Import** is the term the Iranian date trade uses for the hand-sorting and layer-packing process that produces export-grade cartons. Mazafati dates are not uniform off the palm. A harvest contains large premium dates, medium dates, small dates, broken dates, and dates with skin defects. A selective-layering pack is built by hand: the largest, most intact dates are layered flat in the carton, separated by food-grade plastic sheet, so that the buyer opens the carton and finds a visually uniform product suitable for retail repack or premium gift presentation.

A non-layered (often called "bulk loose") pack is the same dates tipped into the carton without sorting or layering. It is cheaper per kilogram and is the correct choice for date paste, date syrup, industrial baking, and animal feed applications. It is the wrong choice for a buyer whose customer is going to open the carton and put the dates on a retail shelf.

The layering labour is a real cost — a skilled packer layers roughly 80-120 kg per day — and it is the line item that distinguishes a $2.80/kg export carton from a $2.20/kg bulk loose carton. We quote both options and document which is being supplied.

## Best Time to Buy Dates from Iran in Bulk: the harvest and price cycle

The **Best Time to Buy Dates from Iran in Bulk** is a question with a precise answer that depends on the buyer's inventory and cash position. The Mazafati harvest runs from August through October, peaking in September. New-crop dates enter the wholesale market in September-October, and pricing is set in those months based on the harvest volume. This is the window in which annual-volume buyers lock contracts.

The complication is that Mazafati is a fresh date, not a dried date. It requires refrigerated storage from harvest through to the final consumer, and its shelf life under correct cold-chain is 6-9 months at -5 to +5°C, longer at deep freeze. A buyer locking a large volume in October needs refrigerated warehouse capacity to hold that volume through the year. A buyer without that capacity should buy in tranches across the year, accepting the carry-stock price uplift from April onwards.

Ramadan is the demand peak. Because the Islamic calendar moves forward roughly 11 days per solar year, Ramadan's position relative to the August-October harvest shifts annually, and the wholesale price in the months immediately before Ramadan reflects that positioning. Buyers who can buy and cold-store in November-December routinely capture the best annual price.

We assemble combo containers routinely and the documentation is structured so that each SKU is a separate invoice line for the buyer's customs broker.

## Cold-chain logistics for Mazafati

Mazafati dates must ship refrigerated. Our standard cold-chain specification for Mazafati export is a reefer container set to 0-5°C for the GCC and South Asia routes (transit 7-14 days) and -5 to 0°C for longer European and East Asian routes (transit 25-40 days) where the deeper set-point extends shelf life on arrival. The container's data logger is read at destination and the temperature record is part of the documentation set.
