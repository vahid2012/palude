Mazafati is the Iranian date variety that cannot be shipped without refrigeration. Unlike dried dates — which are shelf-stable at ambient temperature for months — Mazafati is a soft, fresh date with 20-26% moisture, and its shelf life is measured in weeks at ambient temperature, months at chilled temperature, and a year-plus at deep freeze. For an international buyer, the cold-chain is not a logistics option; it is the determinant of whether the dates arrive at destination in saleable condition. This guide covers the reefer container set-points, transit windows, consolidation rules, packaging formats and destination-port reception that define a successful Mazafati date shipment from Iran.

## Why Mazafati requires cold-chain and dried dates do not

The difference between a fresh Mazafati and a dried date is moisture content and water activity. A dried date at 10-15% moisture has a water activity below the threshold at which mould, yeast and bacterial growth can occur — it is shelf-stable. A Mazafati at 22-26% moisture has a water activity that supports microbial growth and enzymatic browning at ambient temperature, and its quality degrades visibly within 7-14 days at 25-35°C (the temperature range a container encounters in the Gulf, the Indian Ocean, or a non-reefer hold). Under refrigeration at 0-5°C, the same date holds its quality for 6-9 months. At deep freeze (-18°C), it holds for 18+ months.

This is why the [Mazafati dates bulk import](/export/mazafati-dates-bulk-import/) trade is entirely a reefer trade. A buyer who ships Mazafati in a dry container to save the reefer cost will receive a container of fermented, browned, unsaleable dates — the reefer cost is not an optional add-on, it is the cost of the product arriving as the product.

## Reefer container set-points by destination

The correct reefer set-point depends on the transit time and the buyer's storage plan at destination. The set-points we use:

- **GCC destinations (transit 7-14 days)**: set-point 0 to +5°C. The short transit window allows a chilled (not frozen) set-point, and the dates arrive at a temperature suitable for immediate chilled distribution. This is the default for UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Kuwait and Oman destinations.
- **South Asia destinations (transit 10-18 days)**: set-point 0 to +5°C, with a dehumidification setting to manage condensation on arrival in humid ports. The default for India, Pakistan and Bangladesh.
- **European destinations (transit 25-40 days via Suez)**: set-point -5 to 0°C. The deeper set-point extends shelf life on arrival, because European distribution chains are longer and the dates may sit in chilled warehouse storage for weeks before retail dispatch.
- **East Asian destinations (transit 30-45 days)**: set-point -5 to 0°C, with the same shelf-life logic as the European route.

The set-point is recorded on the reefer's data logger, and the logger record is part of the documentation set the buyer's broker presents at destination. A temperature excursion (the reefer losing set-point during transit) is visible on the logger and is the basis for an insurance claim if the cargo has degraded.

## The consolidation rules: what can and cannot share a reefer

A buyer consolidating Mazafati dates with other Iranian food exports in a single reefer container needs to understand the set-point conflicts. The rules we apply:

- **Dates and pistachios**: do not consolidate in the same reefer. Pistachios ship dry at ambient temperature; at 0°C the pistachios are over-chilled (not damaged, but the condensation on arrival in a humid port is a mould risk), and at ambient the dates degrade. Ship dates in the reefer, pistachios in a dry container.
- **Dates and almonds**: same conflict as dates and pistachios. Almonds are a dry cargo; do not consolidate with refrigerated dates.
- **Dates and saffron**: do not consolidate. Saffron is exquisitely sensitive to moisture and odour absorption; a refrigerated date cargo releases moisture that the saffron will absorb, degrading the saffron's aroma. Saffron ships in a dry container.
- **Dates and dried fruit (figs, apricots, raisins)**: consolidation is possible if the dried fruit is low-moisture (under 15%) and the set-point is acceptable to both. The dried fruit does not require refrigeration but tolerates it; the dates require it. We consolidate low-moisture dried fruit with Mazafati in a reefer set to the date set-point.

The general rule: refrigerate the cargo that requires refrigeration, and ship the dry cargo dry. A buyer who insists on consolidating everything into one reefer to save one container's freight is a buyer who eventually absorbs the cost of the cargo that should have been dry.

## Packaging formats for Mazafati reefer export

The packaging format inside the reefer determines how the cold air circulates and how the dates present on arrival:

- **Selective-layered 10 kg cartons**: the premium export format. Dates are hand-layered flat with separator sheets; the carton is vented to allow cold air circulation. 700-1,000 cartons per 20ft reefer, depending on stacking. This is the format for retail-repack and gift-grade buyers.
- **Bulk loose 10-12 kg cartons**: dates unsorted, tipped into the carton. Lower per-kg cost; the format for date paste, syrup and industrial buyers. Same venting, same cartons-per-container.
- **Gift trays and retail clamshells**: 1-5 kg formats for buyers who sell the dates in the packaging they ship in. Higher packaging cost; requires careful stacking to avoid crushing in transit.
- **Industrial paste drums**: 50 kg pails or 200 kg drums for date paste buyers. Not a reefer cargo — date paste is produced from dates and is shelf-stable; it ships in a dry container.

## Transit windows and the harvest-cycle timing

The Mazafati harvest runs August through October, peaking in September. New-crop dates enter the export market in September-October. The transit windows from Bandar Abbas (Iran's main Gulf export port) are:

- **Bandar Abbas to Jebel Ali (UAE)**: 5-7 days
- **Bandar Abbas to Mundra / Nhava Sheva (India)**: 10-14 days
- **Bandar Abbas to Hamburg / Rotterdam**: 25-35 days via Suez
- **Bandar Abbas to Shanghai / Qingdao**: 30-40 days
- **Overland to Moscow via Azerbaijan**: 10-14 days (reefer truck, not container)

The implication for a buyer is that a Mazafati order shipped from Bandar Abbas in October arrives in Hamburg in mid-November. A buyer running a Ramadan retail programme needs to back-calculate from Ramadan's expected date to the required arrival date, then subtract the transit window, then subtract the lead time from proforma to vessel sailing — and place the order early enough to hit the window. Ramadan is the demand peak, and the buyers who wait until Ramadan is eight weeks out are the buyers who pay the pre-Ramadan carry-stock premium.

## Destination port reception

Not every destination port accepts reefer containers with the same ease. The questions to confirm before contracting:

- **Reefer plug availability**: the destination port must have working reefer plugs to hold the container between discharge and gate-out. Some smaller ports have limited plug capacity and a reefer may wait on a vessel for plug availability.
- **Reefer handling charges**: destination ports charge a reefer surcharge above the standard container handling fee; this is a landed-cost line item.
- **Cold-storage availability at destination**: if the buyer cannot immediately receive the container into chilled storage, the container must stay plugged in at the port, accruing daily plug charges. Confirm cold-storage reception before the vessel sails.

## Insurance and the temperature logger

Reefer cargo insurance is not standard general-cargo insurance. We arrange transit insurance through a London-market specialist marine cargo policy that covers temperature-excursion damage — the loss that occurs when a reefer loses set-point in transit and the cargo degrades even though the container is physically intact. The reefer's data logger is the evidence of the temperature history, and the logger record is the document that supports a temperature-excursion claim.

A buyer who ships Mazafati under a general cargo policy is a buyer whose temperature-excursion loss is not covered — and temperature excursion is the single most common loss event in reefer date shipping.
