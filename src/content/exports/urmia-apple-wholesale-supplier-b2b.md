## Cold storage Urmia apple export: the year-round supply infrastructure

The single most important piece of infrastructure in the Urmia apple supply chain is cold storage. The Urmia apple harvest is concentrated in a 10-week window from late September through December, but the export trade runs for 7-8 months — from the harvest through May. This is possible only because the Urmia apple industry has invested in controlled-atmosphere (CA) and refrigerated cold-storage facilities that hold the apples at 0-2°C with regulated oxygen and CO₂ levels, slowing the respiration and the softening that would otherwise end the apple's shelf life within weeks of harvest.

Palude's **cold storage Urmia apple export** capability is the infrastructure that allows us to offer year-round supply to international buyers. The cold-storage facilities hold the apples under controlled atmosphere from the harvest through the spring, and the fruit is drawn down from storage as container orders are contracted. For a buyer, the practical implication is that an Urmia apple sourcing programme is not constrained to the harvest window — a buyer can contract monthly container shipments from October through May, with the cold-stored fruit matching the fresh fruit's firmness and colour to within measurable tolerance.

The cold-storage discipline is what separates a wholesale supplier from a harvest-season shipper. A harvest-season shipper can supply October through December; a wholesale supplier with cold storage can supply October through May. When a buyer is evaluating **Urmia apple wholesale supplier** options, the first question to ask is whether the supplier holds cold-storage capacity — because a supplier without cold storage is a supplier who cannot ship after December, and a supplier who cannot ship after December is not a year-round sourcing partner.

## The cold-chain workflow from orchard to container

The cold-chain workflow is the sequence of temperature-controlled steps that protect the apple from the orchard to the destination port. A break in the cold chain — a period where the fruit is held at ambient temperature — is the cause of the softening, colour loss and decay that produce a container rejection at destination. The Palude cold-chain workflow is:

1. **Harvest to cold store**: apples are picked, field-sorted, and transported to the cold-storage facility within hours of harvest. The fruit is cooled to 0-2°C within 24 hours of picking, arresting the respiration that would otherwise begin to soften the flesh.
2. **Cold-storage hold**: the apples are held under controlled atmosphere (CA) at 0-2°C, with oxygen reduced to 1-2% and CO₂ raised to 2-3%, slowing the respiration to a near-dormant rate. Under CA, Red Delicious holds its firmness above 6 kg/cm² for 6-8 months.
3. **Grading and packing**: the fruit is graded, sized and packed at the cold-storage facility, with the packed cartons returned to cold storage pending container loading. The grading and packing are done in a temperature-controlled environment to prevent the fruit from warming during the packing process.
4. **Container pre-cooling and loading**: the reefer container is pre-cooled to the set-point (0-2°C for Urmia apples) before loading. The packed cartons are loaded into the pre-cooled container, and the reefer is sealed with the data logger running.
5. **Transit**: the reefer maintains the set-point through the transit, with the data logger recording the temperature history. For overland shipments to Russia, the reefer truck maintains the same set-point.
6. **Destination**: the container is plugged in at the destination port and held at set-point until customs clearance and delivery to the buyer's cold storage.

A break at any step — a delay in field-to-cold-store transport, a packing facility at ambient temperature, a container that is not pre-cooled — produces a container that arrives below spec. The cold-chain discipline is the supplier's responsibility, and it is the discipline that distinguishes a wholesale supplier from a harvest-season shipper.

## Urmia apple packaging for export: the format and stowage detail

The **Urmia apple packaging for export** decision is a destination-channel decision, and the format and the stowage pattern together determine how the container arrives. The packaging formats available from the Urmia packing facilities are:

1. **2-row baskets**: apples layered in two rows inside a rigid basket. The 2-row layout is used for larger fruit (counts 80-113) where presentation matters. Available in 4 kg, 6 kg and 10 kg basket sizes. The 2-row basket is the retail-ready format for Gulf and South Asian convenience retail, where the basket is sold from the shelf.
2. **3-row baskets**: a denser pack for medium-sized fruit (counts 125-150). The 3-row basket is used when the destination retail channel sells from the basket and the per-unit cost must be kept low. Available in 4 kg, 6 kg and 10 kg basket sizes.
3. **4 kg baskets**: the small retail format, typically 2-row, for the convenience retail channel. Stackable, ventilated, labelled with variety, count, origin and batch number.
4. **6 kg baskets**: the medium retail format, used in the Gulf and South Asian retail chains that sell apples by weight from the basket.
5. **10 kg baskets**: the wholesale format for distributors who break the basket down into smaller packs at the destination. The 10 kg basket is the workhorse of the Russia and CIS trade, where the destination distributor repacks into the local retail format.
6. **Plastic boxes**: the returnable-packaging format, used where the destination channel operates a returnable-crate system. Plastic boxes are more expensive per unit but are recovered and reused, reducing the per-trip packaging cost for a recurring programme.
7. **Cardboard boxes**: the one-way export format, with trays or bulk pack inside a printed or plain corrugated box. Cardboard boxes are the default for supermarket-channel export, where the box is the shelf display unit.

The container-load stowage is the second half of the packaging decision. Apples are a respiring cargo, and the reefer's airflow must circulate through the cargo to remove the respiratory heat and the ethylene the fruit produces. A container stowed too tightly — with cartons packed flush against the walls and the ceiling — blocks the airflow and produces temperature gradients. A container stowed too loosely — with gaps between carton stacks — allows the air to short-circuit and leaves pockets of warm fruit. The correct stowage is a tight-but-ventilated pattern, with cartons stacked on pallets or in a block-stow with the ventilation holes aligned, and with a clearance of 10-15 cm between the cargo and the reefer's evaporator unit.

## The documentation set for an Iranian apple B2B import

An **Iranian apple B2B import** engagement is a documented transaction, and the document set is what the buyer's bank pays against (under a letter of credit) and what the destination customs authority clears against. Every Urmia apple container from Palude ships with:

- **Phytosanitary Certificate** from the Iranian Plant Protection Organization, confirming the fruit is free of quarantine pests and compliant with the destination's plant-health requirements. This is the document the destination's plant-protection authority requires for fresh-fruit import clearance.
- **Certificate of Origin** from the Iran Chamber of Commerce, confirming Iranian origin. This is the document that determines the destination's applicable tariff rate and, in some jurisdictions, the eligibility for preferential treatment.
- **Packing list and commercial invoice** matching the bill of lading line by line, with count, net weight and gross weight per carton and per pallet.
- **Bill of lading** (sea freight via Bandar Abbas) or **CMR consignment note** (overland to Russia and the CIS) or **airway bill** (urgent sample shipments). The transport document is also the document of title for letter-of-credit transactions.
- **Reefer set-point record and data-logger download** where the destination or the buyer's insurer requires temperature-history evidence. The data-logger record is the document that supports a temperature-excursion claim under a specialist marine cargo policy.
- **Halal certificate** on request for GCC-bound shipments, though fresh fruit is generally halal by category and the certificate is a formality.

Destination-specific add-ons — EAC conformity declaration for Russia and the EAEU, additional third-party pre-shipment inspection for government-tender buyers, GMO-free declaration where required — are quoted as line items. ## Payment channels for Iranian apple exports

Iranian banks are not directly connected to the European or US clearing systems, so direct EUR or USD payments to an Iranian account are generally not possible. The working payment channels for Urmia apple exports are the same channels that govern all Iranian exports: a third-country correspondent bank transfer, a letter of credit issued by the buyer's bank and confirmed through a third-country correspondent, or an escrow arrangement for established relationships. For first-time relationships and container-scale shipments, we recommend a letter of credit; for established relationships, a third-country T/T is the most common channel. The full payment-channel framework is covered in our [payment methods for Iranian exports](/blog/payment-methods-iranian-exports-b2b/) guide, and we confirm the working payment channel with the buyer's bank before issuing a proforma.
