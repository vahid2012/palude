Urmia, in Iran's West Azerbaijan province, is the country's single most productive apple-growing region. The Urmia plain and the surrounding mountain slopes hold over 21,000 hectares of apple orchards and produce more than 450,000 tonnes of apples each year — a volume that places Urmia at the centre of the Iranian apple trade and makes it one of the largest apple-producing districts in the Middle East. ## Why Urmia is Iran's leading apple-producing region

Urmia's position as Iran's top apple-producing region is the product of three factors that converge in a single geography. The first is altitude: Urmia sits at roughly 1,330 metres above sea level, and the apple orchards climb the surrounding slopes to 1,800 metres. This elevation produces the diurnal temperature variation — warm days and cool nights — that drives sugar accumulation, skin colour development, and the firm flesh texture that distinguishes mountain apples from lowland fruit. The second is soil: the alluvial plain around Lake Urmia provides the well-drained, slightly acidic loam that apple trees prefer. The third is water: the snow-fed rivers descending from the surrounding mountains deliver the reliable irrigation the orchards need through the dry Iranian summer.

The result is an apple with characteristics that international buyers specifically request: a firm, dense flesh that holds through cold storage and shipping, a skin colouration that reaches 70% and above on export-grade fruit, and a sugar-acid balance that holds up in both fresh retail and processing applications. When a buyer specifies an **Iranian apple supplier Urmia** origin on a proforma, they are specifying these mountain-apple characteristics — not just a geographic label.

## The dominant varieties: Red Delicious and Golden Delicious

Over 90% of Urmia's apple production is in two varieties: Red Delicious and Golden Delicious. Red Delicious is the variety that built the Urmia apple export trade — the dark-red, elongated, slightly striped apple that Russian and Central Asian buyers have imported from Iran for decades. Golden Delicious is the yellow-gold counterpart, with a thinner skin and a sweeter, more aromatic profile that appeals to Gulf and South Asian retail channels. For a buyer evaluating **bulk Iranian apples for import**, the Red Delicious versus Golden Delicious decision is primarily a destination-market decision: Russia and the CIS favour Red Delicious; the Gulf and South Asia take both; processing buyers take whichever variety is priced better at the time of contracting.

Beyond the two dominant varieties, Urmia's orchards also produce smaller volumes of Gala, Granny Smith, and Delbar Stival (a local selection). These are available on request but are not the volume varieties — a buyer specifying Gala or Granny Smith should expect a longer lead time and a smaller pool of suppliers. ## The harvest cycle and the cold-storage supply window

The Urmia apple harvest runs from late September through December. The Red Delicious harvest typically begins in the last week of September and runs through October; Golden Delicious follows in October-November; the later varieties and the high-mountain orchards harvest into December. This is the window when fresh-picked apples are available at the lowest cost of the year, and it is the window when a buyer building an annual sourcing programme should lock volume.

The critical feature of the Urmia apple supply cycle is that exports do not stop when the harvest ends. Urmia's apple industry has invested heavily in cold-storage facilities — controlled-atmosphere and refrigerated stores that hold the apples at 0-2°C with regulated oxygen and CO₂ levels. These facilities preserve the fruit's firmness, colour and sugar content for months after harvest, and they are the reason **Urmia apple export wholesale** shipments continue from the harvest through May. For a buyer, this means the Iranian apple supply is not a 10-week-a-year category; it is a 7-8-month category, with cold-stored fruit shipping from January through May at a quality level that approaches fresh-picked.

## Export-grade specifications: what to write into a proforma

An export-grade Urmia apple is defined by measurable specifications, and a buyer who writes these into the proforma receives the fruit they contracted. The core export-grade specifications are:

- **Colour**: 70% or greater skin colouration for Red Delicious; the deep red blush that Russian and Gulf retail channels expect. Golden Delicious is graded on a clean, uniform yellow-gold surface with minimal russeting.
- **Firmness**: the mountain-apple flesh texture that holds through cold storage and the 25-40 day transit to distant destinations. Measured by penetrometer; export-grade Red Delicious should test at 7-8 kg/cm² at harvest and hold above 6 kg/cm² through the cold-storage window.
- **Size**: graded by count per carton — 80, 88, 100, 113, 125, 138, 150, 163, 175. The 100-138 range is the export standard for most destinations; larger counts (80-100) command a premium in Gulf retail; smaller counts (150-175) serve the processing and price-sensitive retail channels.
- **Brix**: 12-14% for Red Delicious, 13-15% for Golden Delicious, measured by refractometer at the packing facility.
- **Defect tolerance**: maximum 5% defective fruit at loading, including bruises, scab, insect damage and colour shortfalls. A buyer who specifies a tighter tolerance (2-3%) pays a premium but receives a cleaner pack.

A proforma that names these specifications is a proforma that is enforceable. A proforma that simply says "Red Delicious apples, export grade" leaves every one of these variables to the supplier's interpretation, and the interpretation that favours the supplier is never the interpretation that favours the buyer.

## Packaging formats for Urmia apple export

The Urmia apple industry packs for export in several formats, and the choice is a destination-channel decision:

1. **2-row baskets**: the traditional Urmia apple pack, with apples layered in two rows inside a rigid basket. Common in 4 kg, 6 kg and 10 kg basket sizes. The 2-row layout is used for larger fruit (counts 80-113) where presentation matters.
2. **3-row baskets**: a denser pack for medium-sized fruit (counts 125-150), used when the destination retail channel sells from the basket and the per-unit cost must be kept low.
3. **4 kg and 6 kg baskets**: the retail-ready formats for Gulf and South Asian convenience retail. The baskets are stackable, ventilated, and labelled with variety, count, origin and batch number.
4. **10 kg baskets**: the wholesale format for distributors who break the basket down into smaller packs at the destination. The 10 kg basket is the workhorse of the Russia and CIS trade.
5. **Plastic and cardboard boxes**: the supermarket-channel format, with trays or bulk pack inside a printed or plain corrugated box. Plastic boxes are used where returnable packaging is the destination standard; cardboard boxes are the default for one-way export.

The packaging format is quoted as a line item on the proforma, and the per-carton cost varies meaningfully across formats. The [Urmia apple packaging for export](/export/urmia-apple-wholesale-supplier-b2b/) page covers the packaging specifications in detail.

## Cold-chain logistics and the HS code

Urmia apples ship under HS code **08133000** (apples, dried — note: fresh apples ship under HS 080810, which is the fresh-apple HS code; the 08133000 reference in some Iranian trade documentation refers to the dried-apple category and should be verified against your destination's tariff schedule before contracting). Fresh Urmia apples ship in reefer containers at 0-2°C, with the reefer set-point and the data-logger record documented in the shipment specification. The transit-time and reefer-set-point framework is the same framework our [shipping dates from Iran cold-chain](/blog/shipping-dates-from-iran-cold-chain/) guide covers for date exports, and the principle is identical: refrigerate the cargo, document the temperature history, and insure against temperature-excursion damage.

The exit port for Urmia apples depends on the destination. For Russia and the CIS, the overland route from Urmia through Azerbaijan and across the Caspian is the fastest, with a 10-14 day transit to Moscow. For Gulf destinations, Bandar Abbas is the exit port, with a 5-10 day transit to Jebel Ali and Dammam. For South Asia, Bandar Abbas to Mundra and Nhava Sheva runs 10-14 days. For European destinations, Bandar Abbas via Suez runs 25-35 days, and the longer transit makes the cold-storage firmness specification critical. ## The documentation set

Every Urmia apple export from Palude ships with the following document set:

- **Phytosanitary Certificate** issued by the Iranian Plant Protection Organization, confirming the fruit is free of quarantine pests and compliant with the destination's plant-health requirements.
- **Certificate of Origin** issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce, confirming Iranian origin — the document that determines the destination's applicable tariff rate.
- **Packing list and commercial invoice** matching the bill of lading line by line, with count, net weight and gross weight per carton.
- **Bill of lading** (sea) or CMR consignment note (overland to Russia and the CIS) or airway bill (urgent sample shipments).
- **Reefer set-point record and data-logger download** where the destination or the buyer's insurer requires temperature-history evidence.
- **Halal certificate** on request for GCC-bound shipments, though fresh fruit is generally halal by category and the certificate is a formality.

## Minimum order, pricing and lead time

- **Minimum order**: 1 pallet (approximately 1,000 kg) for trial shipments; 1x20ft reefer container (approximately 10-12 metric tons) for standard export; 1x40ft reefer (approximately 20-24 metric tons) for full-container consolidation.
- **Pricing**: quoted per kilogram in USD, FOB or CIF to the destination port. Pricing is harvest-cycle dependent — the lowest prices are available October-December during the fresh harvest, and cold-stored fruit from January onwards carries a storage premium.
- **Lead time**: 10-14 business days from proforma acceptance for stock-grade fruit in the harvest window; 14-21 days for cold-stored fruit; 21-28 days for bespoke sizing and packaging.
- **Incoterms**: FOB Bandar Abbas or the overland origin point; CFR or CIF destination port; DAP to the buyer's warehouse for the overland Russia route.
