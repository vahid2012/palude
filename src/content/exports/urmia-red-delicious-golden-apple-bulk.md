## Red Delicious: the variety that built the Urmia export trade

Red Delicious is the apple variety that built the Urmia apple export trade, and it remains the variety Russian, Central Asian and Gulf buyers ask for by name. The Urmia Red Delicious is a dark-red, elongated apple with a slightly striped skin and a firm, crisp flesh. The mountain-grown Red Delicious — from the orchards above 1,500 metres on the slopes surrounding the Urmia plain — develops the deepest colour and the firmest flesh, because the cool nights at altitude drive anthocyanin production (the red pigment) and slow the respiration that softens the fruit.

For a buyer contracting **Urmia Red Delicious apple bulk** supply, the varietal characteristics that matter at the destination are:

- **Colour**: export-grade Red Delicious requires 70% or greater skin colouration. The deep red blush is the visual cue the destination retail channel uses to judge quality, and a container that lands with 50% colour shortfalls is a container that sells at a discount. The mountain-apple colour advantage is why **Iranian mountain apple export** commands a premium over lowland Iranian apple supply.
- **Firmness**: export-grade Red Delicious should test at 7-8 kg/cm² by penetrometer at harvest and hold above 6 kg/cm² through the cold-storage window. The firmness is what allows the apple to survive a 25-40 day reefer transit to Europe or a 10-14 day overland transit to Moscow without bruising and softening.
- **Size**: Red Delicious is graded by count per carton — 80, 88, 100, 113, 125, 138, 150, 163, 175. The 100-138 range is the export standard; the 80-100 range commands a Gulf retail premium; the 150-175 range serves processing and price-sensitive retail.
- **Brix**: 12-14%, measured by refractometer. Red Delicious is not a high-sugar apple; its profile is a moderate sugar with a mild acid balance that suits fresh eating rather than processing.
- **Shape**: the characteristic five-lobed calyx and elongated body. A buyer who specifies "typical Red Delicious shape" is excluding the off-type fruit that some lower-altitude orchards produce.

## Golden Delicious: the sweet, aromatic counterpart

Golden Delicious is the second dominant Urmia variety, and the one that Gulf and South Asian retail channels increasingly prefer. The Urmia Golden Delicious is a yellow-gold apple with a thinner skin than Red Delicious, a sweeter and more aromatic profile, and a flesh that is slightly less firm but holds well in cold storage. For a buyer contracting **Golden Delicious Iranian apple wholesale** supply, the varietal characteristics are:

- **Colour**: a clean, uniform yellow-gold surface with minimal russeting (the brown corky patches that appear on some Golden Delicious fruit). Export-grade Golden Delicious should show less than 10% russeting on the exposed face.
- **Firmness**: 6-7 kg/cm² at harvest, holding above 5 kg/cm² through the cold-storage window. Golden Delicious is slightly softer than Red Delicious, and the transit-time and reefer-set-point specification should reflect this.
- **Size**: graded by the same count-per-carton system as Red Delicious. Golden Delicious tends to size up slightly larger than Red Delicious at the same orchard, so the 88-125 range is the common export grade.
- **Brix**: 13-15%, the higher sugar that makes Golden Delicious the variety for buyers serving a sweet-toothed retail channel.
- **Aroma**: the floral, pear-like aroma that distinguishes Golden Delicious from Red Delicious. The aroma is the selling point in Gulf retail, where the apple is often marketed on fragrance as much as appearance.

The Red Delicious versus Golden Delicious decision is, in practice, a destination-market decision. Russia and the CIS take Red Delicious overwhelmingly. The Gulf takes both, with Golden Delicious gaining share in the UAE and Saudi retail chains. South Asia takes both, with Golden Delicious preferred in the premium retail segment. Processing buyers — juice, canning, concentrate — take whichever variety is priced better at contracting, because the sugar-acid difference is marginal in a processed product.

A 20ft reefer container holds approximately 10-12 metric tons of apples; a 40ft reefer holds approximately 20-24 metric tons. The per-kilogram cost falls as the order moves from pallet to 20ft to 40ft, because the fixed costs — cold-storage handling, documentation, port handling — are spread across a larger volume.

## The grading and sizing framework in detail

Urmia apples are graded on three axes: colour, size and defect tolerance. The colour grade is expressed as a percentage of surface colouration (70%+ for export-grade Red Delicious; clean uniform gold for Golden Delicious). The size grade is expressed as count per carton, with the count inversely proportional to the apple size — count 80 means 80 apples per carton (large fruit), count 175 means 175 apples per carton (small fruit). The defect tolerance is the maximum percentage of fruit that may fall outside the colour and size spec at loading.

A proforma that specifies all three grades is a proforma that is enforceable at destination inspection. A proforma that specifies only "Red Delicious, export grade" leaves the colour percentage, the size count and the defect tolerance to the supplier's interpretation — and the interpretation that is cheapest for the supplier is the interpretation that is most expensive for the buyer at the destination.

## The cold-storage supply window and pricing through the season

The Urmia apple harvest runs late September through December, and the cold-storage facilities in Urmia hold the fruit from harvest through May. This creates two pricing windows. The first is the harvest window (October-December), when fresh-picked fruit is abundant and pricing is at its lowest. The second is the cold-storage window (January-May), when the fruit is held in controlled-atmosphere storage and pricing carries a storage premium that rises as the stored volume depletes.

For a buyer building an annual sourcing programme, the strategic decision is how much volume to lock in the harvest window versus how much to defer to the cold-storage window. The harvest window offers the lowest price but requires the buyer to take or store the volume; the cold-storage window offers flexibility but at a rising premium. A common programme structure is to lock 60-70% of annual volume in the harvest window and hold the remaining 30-40% as cold-storage drawdown through the spring.

## Iranian mountain apple export: the premium tier

Within the Urmia apple supply, the mountain-grown fruit — from the high-altitude orchards on the slopes above the Urmia plain — represents the premium tier. The mountain apples develop deeper colour, firmer flesh and higher sugar than the plain-grown fruit, because the cool nights and the intense UV at altitude drive the pigment and sugar accumulation that define the premium profile. A buyer who specifies **Iranian mountain apple export** is specifying this premium tier, and the proforma should name the altitude band (typically 1,500-1,800 metres) and the colour and firmness specs that distinguish the mountain fruit from the plain fruit.

The mountain-apple premium is real and defensible at the destination: a container of mountain-grown Red Delicious lands at the destination port with the colour and firmness that the Gulf retail channel pays a premium for, and the per-kilogram price difference is recovered at the destination shelf. For buyers serving a premium retail channel, the mountain-apple specification is the one that protects the destination margin.

## Packaging for container-scale shipments

Container-scale shipments pack in the same formats as pallet-scale — 2-row and 3-row baskets, 4 kg / 6 kg / 10 kg baskets, plastic and cardboard boxes — but the container-load packaging decision also considers the stowage pattern, the ventilation, and the reefer airflow. Apples are a respiring cargo, and a container that is stowed too tightly or too loosely will develop temperature gradients that produce condensation and decay.
