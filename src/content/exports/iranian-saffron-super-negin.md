## Why Super Negin Iranian Saffron is the export grade buyers ask for

Saffron grading in Iran is ultimately about how much of the red stigma is present and how little of the yellow style remains. **Super Negin Iranian Saffron** is the longest, thickest, all-red thread grade: the entire stigma is harvested with no yellow tail, the threads are visually uniform, and the crocin (colour), picrocrocin (bitterness) and safranal (aroma) values are the highest the plant produces. It is the grade European food manufacturers specify when the finished product is a premium retail jar, a dairy colouring application, or a pharma-grade extract.

For bulk buyers the practical difference between grades is dramatic. A 1 kg order of Super Negin, packed in vacuum-sealed food-grade aluminium-lined pouches under argon, will hold its ISO 3632 values for 24-30 months stored correctly. A poorly trimmed Pushal grade, by contrast, begins to lose safranal within months and arrives at the destination port already below spec. When you place a bulk Iranian saffron supply order, the grade name on the proforma is not a courtesy label — it is the single biggest determinant of what you can resell the material for.

## Iranian Sargol Saffron Bulk: the all-red standard for industrial buyers

**Iranian Sargol Saffron Bulk** is the second grade importers should understand. Sargol (literally "top of the flower") is the all-red tip of the stigma, trimmed away from the rest of the thread. It is shorter than Super Negin and Negin but offers the same high crocin reading because there is no yellow style at all. For industrial buyers who need colour strength per gram rather than visual thread length — dairy, confectionery, beverage colouring, supplement encapsulation — Sargol is usually the most cost-effective choice because you pay for pigment, not for the visual presentation a retail jar demands.

The trap buyers fall into is accepting "Sargol" on a proforma without a lab certificate. A trimmed Pushal that has been cut to remove most — but not all — of the yellow will visually pass as Sargol to an untrained eye, yet its ISO 3632 crocin reading will land in the 180-200 range rather than the 230+ that genuine Sargol delivers. This is why every Palude saffron shipment ships with an ISO 3632 certificate from an accredited Iranian laboratory and a sample retained at our facility for dispute resolution.

## The ISO 3632 framework: how we verify Bulk Iranian Saffron Supply Grade A++

ISO 3632 is the international standard for saffron specification and test methods. It measures three absorbance values that map directly to the three commercially relevant compounds:

- **Crocin (absorbance at 440 nm)** — colouring strength. The higher the number, the less saffron you need to achieve a target colour in your application. Genuine Super Negin and Sargol test at 230-260+; lower grades sit at 120-190.
- **Picrocrocin (absorbance at 257 nm)** — the bitter, savoury flavour note. Specified at 70+ for Category I (the top ISO grade).
- **Safranal (absorbance at 330 nm)** — the aroma compound. ISO Category I requires 20-50; below 20 the saffron smells flat, above 50 it has been over-dried or adulterated.

When we quote **Bulk Iranian Saffron Supply Grade A++**, we attach the ISO 3632 test report to the proforma. Anything that tests below Category I is relabelled and repriced — it never ships as Grade A++. We also run a moisture test (max 12% under ISO) and a UV fluorescence check to rule out the most common adulterants: dyed corn silk, safflower petals, and gelatine-coated animal fibre. These are the adulteration patterns European customs labs screen for, and a buyer who skips lab verification at origin eventually fails one of those screens.

## Pure Saffron Threads Iranian Negin: when the grade choice is visual

**Pure Saffron Threads Iranian Negin** sits between Super Negin and Sargol. Negin is a long, all-red thread like Super Negin, but it is harvested with a slightly thicker bundle and is not always quite as visually uniform. For retail-pack buyers whose customer opens the jar and judges the saffron by the length and colour of the threads, Negin is the sweet spot: it photographs and displays like Super Negin at a meaningfully lower per-kilo price. For industrial buyers, Negin and Sargol are functionally interchangeable — choose on price and lab values, not on the name.

The decision tree we walk buyers through is simple. If the saffron ends up in a clear glass retail jar or a premium gift set, specify Super Negin or Negin. If it ends up in an opaque industrial pack, a dairy vat, or an extract facility, specify Sargol and buy on crocin. If you are building a [saffron and nuts gift box wholesale](/export/golden-almonds-iran-sourcing/) programme, the visual grade matters — specify Super Negin and accept the premium.

## Packaging, shelf life and the cold-chain question

Saffron does not require cold-chain shipping, but it is ruinously sensitive to moisture, UV and oxygen. Our export packaging standard is:

1. **Primary pack**: food-grade aluminium-lined triple-laminated pouch, nitrogen or argon flush, heat-sealed.
2. **Secondary pack**: labelled carton with batch number, harvest year, ISO 3632 values, and net weight.
3. **Tertiary pack**: corrugated export carton, 1-5 kg per carton, palletised for full-container orders.

Shelf life under this packaging is 24-30 months at ambient warehouse temperature below 25°C, away from direct sunlight. Vacuum sealing is available on request but is not our default — over-vacuum can fracture the threads and reduce the visual grade. For buyers combining saffron with temperature-sensitive goods, a reefer container is the safer consolidation choice, as described in our [shipping dates from Iran cold-chain](/blog/shipping-dates-from-iran-cold-chain/) guide.
