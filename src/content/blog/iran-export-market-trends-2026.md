The Iranian export market in 2026 is shaped by three forces: the harvest-cycle dynamics of the food verticals (saffron, pistachios, dates), the capacity additions in the petrochemical verticals (polyethylene, methanol, urea), and the evolving payment and compliance environment that determines which buyers can transact and on what terms. This guide is our desk's 2026 outlook for the categories we export most, and it is the planning context we share with buyers who are building their annual sourcing programmes.

## Saffron: the 2026 harvest cycle and pricing outlook

The Iranian saffron harvest runs late October through mid-November, and the 2026 harvest will set the pricing for the following 12 months. The structural factors shaping 2026 saffron pricing:

- **Khorasan production**: the Khorasan provinces (Razavi and South) produce over 90% of Iranian saffron, and the harvest volume is the single largest price determinant. A normal-weather harvest produces 350-400 tons; a weather-stressed harvest can drop 15-25%, with a proportional price uplift.
- **Carry-stock**: the volume of unsold saffron carried into the new harvest from the previous season. High carry-stock softens the new-harvest price; low carry-stock amplifies it. The 2025 carry-stock into 2026 is moderate, which suggests a normal-weather 2026 harvest will produce stable-to-modestly-higher pricing.
- **EU and Gulf demand**: European demand for [bulk Super Negin saffron for European importers](/export/iranian-saffron-super-negin/) continues to grow, driven by the premium retail and food-service channels. Gulf demand is stable but Ramadan-cyclical.
- **Labour cost**: saffron is hand-harvested, and Iranian rural labour costs have risen steadily. The labour cost is a structural floor under the saffron price — the price cannot fall below the cost of the harvest labour for a sustained period.

For 2026 buyers, the planning implication is: lock annual volume in the November-January post-harvest window, when fresh stock is abundant and lab values are at their peak. ## Pistachios: the 2026 supply and aflatoxin-policy outlook

The Iranian pistachio harvest runs late August through October, and the 2026 harvest will follow the established Kerman and Rafsanjan cycle. The structural factors:

- **Alternate-bearing biology**: pistachio trees are alternate-bearing — a heavy-crop year is followed by a lighter-crop year. The 2026 harvest's position in the alternate-bearing cycle is a supply determinant that buyers should track, because a light-crop year produces tighter supply and higher pricing.
- **Aflatoxin policy tightening**: the EU's aflatoxin enforcement on pistachios has tightened over the past five years, and Russia's de facto 4 ppb large-importer standard is now tighter than the EU's 10 ppb. Iranian suppliers that have invested in optical sorting and HPLC testing — the suppliers we work with — are the suppliers whose product clears the tightened limits. Suppliers that have not invested are the suppliers whose product is increasingly locked out of the stricter destinations.
- **California competition**: California pistachio production continues to expand, and California origin is the price competitor for Iranian pistachios in the size-driven retail segment. The Iranian competitive edge is flavour and the [most economical Iranian pistachio for industrial buyers](/export/bulk-fandoghi-pistachios-wholesale/) — the Fandoghi variety's kernel yield and flavour profile, not the size competition with California.

For 2026 buyers, the planning implication is: specify the aflatoxin limit in the contract (EU 10 ppb or Russia 4 ppb), require the HPLC certificate at the quote stage, and lock volume in the September-November post-harvest window. The [Iranian pistachio quality standards](/blog/iranian-pistachio-quality-standards-aflatoxin/) guide covers the aflatoxin framework.

## Dates: the 2026 Ramadan positioning

The Iranian Mazafati date trade is Ramadan-cyclical, and the Ramadan date moves forward roughly 11 days per solar year. The 2026 Ramadan positioning — earlier in the solar year than 2025 — means the pre-Ramadan demand peak falls earlier, and the buyers who plan the [Mazafati dates Bam vs Jiroft wholesale](/export/mazafati-dates-bulk-import/) volume for Ramadan retail need to back-calculate from the earlier Ramadan date, subtract the [shipping dates from Iran cold-chain](/blog/shipping-dates-from-iran-cold-chain/) transit window, and place orders earlier in the harvest cycle.

The 2026 Mazafati harvest (August-October 2026) will set the pricing for the 2027 Ramadan, but the carry-stock from the 2025 harvest will set the pricing for the early-2026 Ramadan window. Buyers planning the early-2026 Ramadan should have contracted the carry-stock volume in late 2025; buyers planning the 2027 Ramadan should contract the 2026 new-harvest volume in October-November 2026.

## Petrochemicals: the 2026 capacity additions

Iranian petrochemical capacity continues to expand, with the South Pars phases at Assaluyeh adding methanol and polyolefin capacity. The 2026 capacity picture:

- **Polyethylene**: Iranian LDPE and LLDPE film-grade capacity is stable-to-expanding, and the [Iranian polyethylene film grade supplier](/export/iranian-polyethylene-film-grade/) trade is well-supplied. Pricing tracks the global polyethylene cycle, which is currently in a period of adequate supply and moderate pricing.
- **Methanol**: Iranian methanol capacity is among the world's largest, and [methanol bulk supply Iran origin](/export/iranian-polyethylene-film-grade/) pricing is set by the gas-feedstock cost advantage — Iranian methanol lands at destination competitively against Gulf and Chinese origin.
- **Urea**: Iranian [urea fertilizer export Iran wholesale](/export/iranian-polyethylene-film-grade/) capacity is stable, and pricing tracks the global urea cycle, which is influenced by Indian tender demand, Chinese export policy, and the gas-cost positions of the major producers.

For 2026 buyers, the planning implication is: the petrochemical verticals are well-supplied and pricing is competitive, but the payment-channel environment is the gating factor — a buyer whose bank will not process the payment cannot transact regardless of the pricing. ## Stone and bitumen: the 2026 construction demand

Iranian [direct Iranian sourcing export travertine marble](/export/persian-travertine-marble-supplier/) and bitumen exports track the global construction cycle. The 2026 construction-demand picture is regionally differentiated: Gulf construction is in a multi-year upcycle (Saudi Vision 2030 projects, UAE infrastructure); South Asian construction is steady-to-growing; European construction is soft. The implication for stone and bitumen buyers is that the Gulf destinations are the most demand-active, and the Iranian-origin pricing into the Gulf is competitive because of the short sea-route from Bandar Abbas.

## Persian carpets: the 2026 collector and design market

The Persian carpet trade is bifurcated. The antique and collector segment ([antique pre-1900 Heriz rug](/export/persian-rug-9x12-hand-knotted/), Tabriz silk) is a stable-value market driven by gallery and collector demand, with pricing that is piece-specific rather than market-cyclical. The contemporary and vintage segment ([authentic handwoven vintage Persian rug](/export/persian-rug-9x12-hand-knotted/)) is more cyclical, tracking the interior-design and hospitality-construction demand. For 2026, the contemporary segment is supported by the Gulf hospitality construction cycle.

## The payment and compliance environment in 2026

The payment and compliance environment is the cross-cutting factor that affects every vertical. The working payment channels — third-country correspondent banks, letters of credit, escrow — continue to function, but the specific correspondent jurisdictions and the bank-confirmation timelines shift as sanctions designations and bank policies evolve. The 2026 implication for buyers is: confirm the payment channel with your bank at the start of the annual sourcing programme, not order-by-order, because a bank's Iranian-payment policy can shift mid-year and a mid-year policy shift can stall an in-process shipment. The [Iran export documentation checklist](/blog/iran-export-documentation-checklist/) covers the documentation that supports each payment channel.

## Logistics: the 2026 freight and container picture

Ocean freight rates from Bandar Abbas have normalised from the 2021-2023 peak, and container availability is adequate. The reefer market for [Mazafati dates](/export/mazafati-dates-bulk-import/) and other refrigerated cargoes is tighter than the dry-container market, and reefer rates are more seasonal — the pre-Ramadan window is the reefer rate peak. For 2026 buyers, the planning implication is: contract reefer capacity earlier than dry-container capacity, particularly for Ramadan-window shipments.
