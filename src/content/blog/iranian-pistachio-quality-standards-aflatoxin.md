Pistachio quality is the topic where Iranian supplier relationships are made or broken. A buyer who understands the variety grading, the count sizing, the aflatoxin framework and the optical-sorting discipline buys with confidence; a buyer who treats "pistachios" as a commodity and accepts a supplier's grade claim without verification eventually receives a container that fails a destination lab screen. This guide is the quality framework we apply to every [bulk Fandoghi pistachios wholesale](/export/bulk-fandoghi-pistachios-wholesale/) shipment, and it is the framework we recommend buyers require from any Iranian pistachio supplier.

## The four Iranian pistachio varieties and where each fits

Iran produces four commercially distinct pistachio varieties, and each occupies a different price tier and application fit:

- **Kalleh Ghoochi**: large, round, high-yield nut. The variety for buyers who want a visually large pistachio for retail display. Trades at the upper-middle price tier.
- **Akbari**: long, large, light-coloured, the premium retail variety. The highest-price Iranian variety; the one most often counterfeited with cheaper varieties mislabelled as Akbari.
- **Ahmad Aghaei**: long, lighter than Kalleh Ghoochi, mid-tier price. A common retail variety for buyers who want the long shape at a lower price than Akbari.
- **Fandoghi**: small, round, the most abundant and the most economical. The industrial buyer's variety — the lowest cost per kilogram with the flavour and colour profile of Iranian pistachio. The variety we supply most often to [Iranian pistachio supplier for Russia](/export/bulk-fandoghi-pistachios-wholesale/) and to the pistachio butter and paste market.

A buyer's variety choice is an application choice, not a quality choice. A retail buyer wants the large, photogenic Akbari; an industrial buyer wants the economical Fandoghi. The mistake to avoid is specifying a variety without understanding which application it fits — an industrial buyer who pays the Akbari premium for a pistachio butter line is paying for visual characteristics the butter line does not use.

## Count sizing: the number that defines the grade

Within each variety, pistachios are sized by the number of nuts per ounce — the "count." A count of "28-30" means 28-30 nuts per ounce; the smaller the count, the larger the nut. The standard count ranges for Iranian pistachios:

- **Akbari**: 20-22, 22-24, 24-26
- **Kalleh Ghoochi**: 20-22, 22-24, 24-26
- **Ahmad Aghaei**: 22-24, 24-26, 26-28
- **Fandoghi**: 26-28, 28-30, 30-32

The count is a processing-line consideration for industrial buyers (a slicing line calibrated for 28-30 will not run cleanly on 30-32) and a retail-presentation consideration for retail buyers (a 20-22 count looks larger in the jar than a 30-32). The count tolerance — the acceptable variation around the stated range — is typically ±1, and it should be specified on the proforma. A supplier that quotes "Fandoghi 28-30" without stating the tolerance is a supplier whose 28-30 may include a meaningful share of 30-32 nuts.

## Open-mouth versus closed-mouth: the grade distinction that defines the buyer

The single most important grade distinction in Iranian pistachio export is open-mouth (the shell has split along its suture, the nut is ready for hand-snacking) versus closed-mouth (the shell is intact, the nut is destined for industrial shelling). The two grades serve different buyers:

- **Open-mouth**: the retail and food-service grade. The shell is split; the consumer opens the nut by hand. Open-mouth pistachios command a price premium because the retail market demands them.
- **Closed-mouth**: the industrial grade. The shell is intact, protecting the kernel from oxidation and aflatoxin development in transit. Closed-mouth pistachios trade at a discount to open-mouth of the same variety, and are the correct grade for pistachio butter, paste, slicing, and confectionery filling buyers — buyers who are going to shell the nut anyway.

A buyer who buys open-mouth pistachios for an industrial shelling line is paying a premium for a shell-split characteristic the line does not use. A buyer who buys closed-mouth pistachios for a retail hand-snacking line is buying a product the consumer cannot open. The grade choice is the application choice.

## Aflatoxin: the food-safety framework that ends supplier relationships

Aflatoxin is the mycotoxin produced by Aspergillus mould species that can colonise pistachios in the orchard (particularly the "early-split" nuts whose shells open before harvest), during drying, or in storage if moisture is uncontrolled. Aflatoxin B1 is a carcinogen, and the regulatory limits are tight:

- **EU**: 10 ppb total aflatoxin (8 ppb B1) for pistachios destined for direct human consumption; 8 ppb total for ground nuts for further processing.
- **Russia (de facto large-importer standard)**: 4 ppb total — tighter than the EU, reflecting post-recall caution in the Russian trade.
- **GCC**: 10 ppb total (Codex-aligned).
- **USA**: 20 ppb total (FDA action level).

The aflatoxin control protocol we apply has four stages:

1. **Orchard-level early-split removal**: early-split nuts (the highest aflatoxin risk) are removed at harvest, not after drying. The earlier the split is identified and the nut is removed, the lower the lot's aflatoxin profile.
2. **Drying discipline**: nuts are dried to 6-7% moisture within 24 hours of harvest. Below 7% moisture, Aspergillus cannot grow; above it, the mould colonises the nut in storage.
3. **Optical sorting**: every lot passes through an optical sorter that removes stained, insect-damaged and early-split nuts by colour and shape. The optical sorter is the technology that has transformed Iranian pistachio aflatoxin control over the past decade — it removes the high-risk nuts mechanically, at speed, before they enter the export lot.
4. **HPLC testing**: every export lot is sampled and tested by an accredited Iranian laboratory using HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography), the reference method. The HPLC certificate reports total aflatoxin and B1, and the certificate ships with the cargo.

A buyer who accepts "aflatoxin-tested" on a proforma without requiring the HPLC certificate is a buyer whose aflatoxin claim is unverified. The HPLC certificate is the document — not the supplier's assertion.

## The spec sheet a B2B buyer should require

The specification sheet we issue with every pistachio proforma includes:

- **Variety** (Akbari, Kalleh Ghoochi, Ahmad Aghaei, Fandoghi)
- **Count range** (e.g. 28-30) and tolerance (±1)
- **Grade** (open-mouth or closed-mouth)
- **Moisture** (max 6-7%)
- **Aflatoxin** (total and B1, with HPLC certificate attached)
- **Defect tolerance** (broken, stained, insect-damaged, empty — typically capped at 3-5% combined)
- **Packaging** (50 kg PP bags, 25 kg vacuum cartons, 12.5 kg kernel foil pouches)
- **Harvest year** (current crop or carry-stock)
- **Origin** (Kerman / Rafsanjan)

A supplier that cannot produce this spec sheet before contract is a supplier whose grade claims are unverified. The spec sheet is the document that makes the grade real.

## Packaging and the moisture-management question

Pistachio packaging is a moisture-management decision. The options:

- **50 kg PP woven bags (in-shell)**: the default for industrial and Russian export. Breathable, low packaging cost, suitable for dry-container shipping. The bag breathes; the nuts' 6-7% moisture is maintained if the container stays dry.
- **25 kg vacuum-sealed cartons (in-shell or kernel)**: the format for higher-value lots and kernel exports. Vacuum protects the kernel from oxidation; the carton protects the vacuum. Higher packaging cost.
- **12.5 kg foil pouches (kernel)**: the format for the butter, paste and slicing market. Nitrogen-flushed, the kernel arrives in condition for direct processing.

The packaging choice is a destination-climate decision. A buyer shipping to a humid destination port (Mumbai, Jakarta, Lagos) should prefer vacuum or foil packaging to manage the condensation risk; a buyer shipping to a dry destination (Moscow overland, Riyadh) can use the breathable PP bags.
