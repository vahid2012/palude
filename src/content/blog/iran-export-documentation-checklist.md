Documentation is the part of importing from Iran that buyers most often underestimate and that most often causes shipments to stall at destination customs. The document set for an Iranian export is not unusually large by international standards, but it is unforgiving: a missing certificate, a mismatched invoice line, or a payment-channel inconsistency will hold a container at the destination port while the issue is resolved, and port demurrage accrues daily. This checklist is the document framework Palude applies to every export, and it is the framework we recommend international buyers require from any Iranian supplier they evaluate.

## The core document set for every Iranian export

Every shipment from Iran, regardless of product, ships with this baseline document set:

1. **Commercial invoice** — issued by the supplier (Palude), stating the product, grade, quantity, unit price, total value, Incoterms, payment terms, and the buyer's and seller's full details. The commercial invoice must match the packing list and the bill of lading line by line. A mismatch between invoice and packing list is the most common cause of destination customs delays.
2. **Packing list** — detailing every carton, drum, pallet or bag in the shipment: gross weight, net weight, dimensions, and marks/numbers. For multi-SKU consolidated shipments (a dried-fruit combo, a gift-box order), each SKU is a separate packing-list line.
3. **Certificate of Origin** — issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce, Commerce, Industries, Mines and Agriculture, confirming the goods are of Iranian origin. The certificate of origin is the document that determines the destination's applicable tariff rate and, in some jurisdictions, the eligibility for preferential treatment.
4. **Bill of lading** (sea freight) or **airway bill** (air freight) or **CMR** (overland to Russia/CIS) — the transport document issued by the carrier, evidencing receipt of the cargo and the contract of carriage. The bill of lading is also the document of title for letter-of-credit transactions.
5. **MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet)** — for chemical, petrochemical and hazardous cargoes (methanol, bitumen, urea). Non-hazardous food cargoes do not require an MSDS but ship with a product specification sheet instead.

## Product-specific certificates

On top of the core set, each product vertical carries its own certification:

**Saffron** requires an ISO 3632 lab certificate (batch-specific, from an accredited Iranian laboratory), reporting crocin, picrocrocin and safranal absorbance values. The certificate is the only objective evidence of grade, and EU, GCC and ASEAN destination authorities increasingly expect to see it. For saffron destined for the EU, a pesticide residue test report under EU MRLs is also advisable. The [Iranian saffron Super Negin export page](/export/iranian-saffron-super-negin/) covers the ISO 3632 framework in detail.

**Pistachios and nuts** require a phytosanitary certificate from the Iranian Plant Protection Organization and, for most destinations, an aflatoxin test report. The EU's aflatoxin limit for pistachios is 10 ppb total (8 ppb B1); Russia's de facto large-importer standard is 4 ppb total. The HPLC aflatoxin test is run on every export lot, and the test report travels with the phytosanitary certificate. The [bulk Fandoghi pistachios wholesale](/export/bulk-fandoghi-pistachios-wholesale/) page covers the aflatoxin control protocol.

**Dates and dried fruit** require a phytosanitary certificate. For Mazafati dates shipped under cold-chain, the reefer container's temperature logger record is part of the documentation set and is read at destination. The [Mazafati dates bulk import](/export/mazafati-dates-bulk-import/) page covers the date-specific documentation.

**Persian carpets and rugs** require a Cultural Heritage Organization export permit for pieces above the age threshold, and an independent appraisal or age opinion for antique and high-value pieces. The [Persian rug 9x12 hand-knotted](/export/persian-rug-9x12-hand-knotted/) page covers the carpet-specific permits.

**Stone (travertine, marble)** ships with block or slab spec sheets including dimensions, finish, selection grade, and photographs. The [Persian travertine marble supplier](/export/persian-travertine-marble-supplier/) page covers the stone documentation.

**Bitumen** ships with a refinery test certificate (penetration, softening point, ductility, viscosity, flash point, loss on heating, specific gravity) and an MSDS. Bitumen is a non-hazardous cargo under most classifications but ships with the hazardous-cargo declaration where the destination requires it.

**Petrochemicals (polyethylene, methanol, urea)** ship with a refinery certificate of analysis, a technical data sheet, an MSDS, and — for methanol — a hazardous-cargo declaration under IMO Class 3 / UN 1230. The [Iranian polyethylene film grade](/export/iranian-polyethylene-film-grade/) page covers the petrochemical documentation.

## Sanctions-compliant payment routing

Payment is the document-adjacent step that determines whether a shipment can ship at all. Iranian banks are not directly connected to the European or US clearing systems, which means a direct EUR or USD payment from an international buyer to an Iranian supplier account is generally not possible. The working payment channels for Iranian exports are:

- **Third-country correspondent bank**: payment to a correspondent bank in a jurisdiction that clears with both the buyer's country and Iran, with onward transfer to the Iranian supplier. This is the most common channel for European and Gulf buyers.
- **Letter of credit**: an LC issued by the buyer's bank and confirmed through a third-country correspondent bank, payable against the shipping documents. The LC is the channel large buyers use for first-time relationships and high-value shipments, because it protects both sides: the supplier ships against documented terms, the buyer pays against documented shipment.
- **Escrow or counter-trade**: for established relationships and specific jurisdictions, an escrow arrangement or a counter-trade (goods-for-goods) structure.

The payment channel must be confirmed with the buyer's bank before the contract is signed. A buyer who contracts before confirming the payment channel is a buyer who may discover, at the payment stage, that their bank will not process the transaction — and the shipment stalls. We confirm the payment channel as the first step of every engagement, before we issue a proforma.

## Destination-specific add-ons

Beyond the core set and the product-specific certificates, some destinations require additional documentation:

- **EU**: GMO-free declaration for food products, additional third-party lab re-test for Germany-bound food cargoes, EU organic certificate for organic-labelled products, REACH compliance documentation for chemical imports.
- **GCC (Gulf)**: halal certificate for food products, GCC Standardization Organization (GSO) conformity where applicable.
- **Russia / EAEU**: EAC conformity declaration (handled by the Russian importer), phytosanitary re-inspection at the border for plant products.
- **China**: CIF destination port, China CCC certification for certain industrial products, AQSIQ phytosanitary inspection at the Chinese port.
- **India**: BIS certification for certain industrial products, FSSAI registration for food products.

We quote destination-specific add-ons as separate line items so the buyer sees the full documentation cost before contracting.

## Pre-shipment inspection

For government-tender and large-volume buyers, a pre-shipment inspection (PSI) by an international inspection company (SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas, Cotecna) is often a contract requirement. The PSI verifies quantity, quality, packaging and marking before the container ships, and the PSI certificate is the document the buyer's tender authority requires for payment release. We facilitate PSI at the Iranian loading port as part of the export workflow, and the PSI cost is a quoted line item.

## The document-checking workflow

The workflow we recommend for international buyers is:

1. **Before contract**: confirm the payment channel with your bank, confirm the destination's document requirements with your customs broker, and require the supplier's product-specific certificates (ISO 3632, aflatoxin, refinery COA) as part of the price-quote stage.
2. **At contract**: the proforma invoice lists every document the shipment will carry, and the contract makes the documents conditions of payment.
3. **At loading**: the supplier sends the document set (scanned) for the buyer's broker to review before the vessel sails, so any discrepancy is corrected at origin rather than at destination.
4. **At destination**: the originals (or telex-released electronic copies) are presented to destination customs, the cargo is cleared, and the buyer takes delivery.

The step that most often goes wrong is step 3: reviewing the documents at origin before the vessel sails. A buyer who reviews documents only at destination is a buyer who absorbs demurrage while discrepancies are corrected by email with a supplier 4,000 kilometres away. We send the scanned document set for broker review at loading as a default, not as an option.

## Document retention

The full document set for an Iranian import should be retained for a minimum of five years (longer in some jurisdictions — the EU requires 10 years for certain food-safety records). The documents are the evidence of origin, quality and compliance that a destination authority may request in a post-clearance audit, and they are the documents that support a quality dispute resolution under the retained-sample mechanism described in our [how to import Iranian saffron B2B guide](/blog/how-to-import-iranian-saffron-b2b-guide/).
